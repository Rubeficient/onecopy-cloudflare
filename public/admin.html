OneCopy pricing philosophy

Use a consistent rule for routine stock, then reserve human judgment for exceptions. The fields below record distinct kinds of evidence; they are not all interchangeable price tags.

MSRP — the routine baseline

For an ordinary, modern, mass-produced book in As New condition, start at the verified cover price. Do not use MSRP blindly for used, old, scarce, signed, first-edition or unusual copies. Never invent a cover price when it is unknown.

CPV — market evidence

Comparable Price Value is a reference drawn from genuinely similar items. Prefer completed/sold transactions over unsold asking prices. Match edition, format and condition, and consider date, shipping and selling channel.

TPV — target strategy

Target Price Value is OneCopy’s working target asking price: market evidence considered alongside condition, rarity, demand, how quickly the item should sell, acquisition cost and channel fees. This field is a human-entered estimate in this version, not an automatic appraisal.

Confidence — how strong is the evidence?

Confidence describes the evidence, not how much we like the price. Use Low for sparse/weak matches, Medium for some comparable support, and High for several recent, close matches. Keep source notes so estimates can be audited.

Moneyball rule: separate observable facts from assumptions. Track sold price, sell-through, time-to-sale, discount and net proceeds over time; don’t treat a high asking price as proof that an item is worth that amount.
